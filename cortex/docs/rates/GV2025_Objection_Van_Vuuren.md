## SECTION 3: GROUNDS FOR OBJECTION

I, the undersigned registered owner of Erf 6668 Durbanville (9 Carnation Close Weg, Durbanville), hereby formally object to the GV2025 market valuation of **R 2,350,000** as reflected in the 2025 General Valuation Roll.

### 3.1 Basis of Objection

I submit that the GV2025 valuation of R 2,350,000 does not accurately reflect the market value of the subject property as at the prescribed valuation date of **1 July 2025**, and that the correct market value should be no more than **R 2,200,000**.

My objection is grounded in the following:

### 3.2 Inconsistent Valuation Within Carnation Close Weg

The GV2025 valuation of the subject property is **inconsistent with the valuations of directly comparable properties on the same street**. This is the strongest ground for this objection:

| Property | Erf | Erf Size | Dwelling | GV2025 Value | Land Rate (R/m²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **9 Carnation Close Weg (subject)** | **6668** | **360 m²** | **107 m²** | **R 2,350,000** | **R 6,528/m²** |
| 17 Carnation Close Weg | 6660 | 298 m² | 96 m² | R 2,250,000 | R 7,550/m² |
| Other Carnation Close properties | Various | ~460 m² | Various | R 2,240,000 | ~R 4,870/m² |

**Key observations:**

1. **The subject property has been valued R 110,000 higher than the adjacent properties with larger erfs (~460 m²).** Properties at the end of the cul-de-sac, which have erfs approximately 100 m² larger than the subject property, are valued at only R 2,240,000 on the GV2025 roll. There is no justifiable reason why a smaller property (360 m²) should carry a higher valuation than larger properties (460 m²) on the same street with the same zoning, the same configuration, and the same market conditions.

2. **The implied land rate for the subject property (R 6,528/m²) is approximately 34% higher than the land rate for neighbouring properties at R 2,240,000 on ~460 m² (R 4,870/m²).** This disparity is not supported by any property-specific factors that would justify a premium for the subject property — if anything, the subject property's smaller erf size should attract a lower valuation, not a higher one.

3. **No. 17 Carnation Close Weg (Erf 6660) sold for R 2,435,000 in March 2025 but was valued at only R 2,250,000.** By contrast, the subject property sold for R 2,350,000 in February 2024 and was valued at exactly that amount. This demonstrates that the City is not uniformly applying recent sale prices as valuations — No. 17 received a valuation R 185,000 below its actual sale price, while the subject property was valued at its full sale price. This inconsistency is unfair and discriminatory.

### 3.3 Sale Price Does Not Equal Market Value

The GV2025 valuation of R 2,350,000 appears to have been derived directly from the subject property's transfer price of R 2,350,000 (registered 7 February 2024). However:

1. **The Act requires valuation at market value as at 1 July 2025, not at the purchaser's historical transaction price.** The sale occurred approximately 17 months before the valuation date. Market conditions, comparable sales, and property-specific factors must all be considered — not a single transaction in isolation.

2. **A willing buyer, willing seller assessment as at 1 July 2025 must account for comparable valuations on the same street.** If comparable properties with larger erfs are valued at R 2,240,000, a willing buyer would not pay more than R 2,240,000 for a smaller property on the same street with no distinguishing premium features.

3. **No. 17 Carnation Close sold for R 2,435,000 (more than the subject property) yet was valued at R 2,250,000 (less than the subject property).** The City cannot selectively apply sale prices: either sales data governs (in which case No. 17 should be R 2,435,000+) or comparable market analysis governs (in which case the subject property should be aligned with the street at R 2,200,000–R 2,240,000).

### 3.4 Smaller Erf — No Basis for Premium

The subject property has one of the smaller erf sizes on Carnation Close Weg at **360 m²**, compared to the ~460 m² erfs of the properties at the end of the cul-de-sac. In the established property market:

- Smaller erfs in the same street generally attract equal or lower valuations — not higher.
- No structural or locational premium exists for No. 9 that would justify a valuation R 110,000 above properties that are 100 m² larger.
- The dwelling extent (107 m²) is consistent with the modest erf size and does not represent an improvement that would warrant a premium above neighbours.

### 3.5 Excessive Percentage Increase from GV2022

The GV2025 valuation represents a **25% increase** from the GV2022 value of R 1,880,000. While property values have increased in Durbanville over this period, a 25% increase for a 360 m² residential property in a cul-de-sac must be supported by comparable market evidence. Given that larger properties on the same street are valued at R 2,240,000, a 25% increase to R 2,350,000 is not justified.

## SECTION 5: REQUESTED AMENDED VALUATION

Based on the comparable valuation evidence on Carnation Close Weg and the specific factors identified above, I respectfully request that the Municipal Valuer amend the GV2025 valuation of the subject property to:

**R 2,200,000 (Two Million Two Hundred Thousand Rand)**

This figure represents a fair and market-related value as at 1 July 2025, taking into account:

- **Consistency with neighbouring properties:** Adjacent properties with larger erfs (~460 m²) are valued at R 2,240,000. The subject property, with a smaller erf of 360 m², should be valued at or below this level.
- **The principle of uniformity:** Section 44 of the MPRA requires that "the market value of a property [must be] determined by reference to comparable sales." The subject property should not be valued higher than properties with which it is directly comparable — and which have larger erfs.
- **Correcting the sale-price-as-valuation approach:** The valuation should reflect a proper market assessment as at 1 July 2025, not a mechanical adoption of a 17-month-old sale price, particularly when this approach was not uniformly applied to the immediately adjacent property (No. 17, which sold for more but was valued for less).

A valuation of R 2,200,000 would result in a land rate of approximately R 6,111/m², which remains higher than the ~R 4,870/m² rate applied to the larger adjacent properties — a conservative and reasonable request.
