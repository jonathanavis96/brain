Introduction
What is SEO and why does it matter for selling art?

SEO stands for "Search Engine Optimisation." In plain English, it means making your website easier for Google to find and understand — so that when someone searches for "buy South African art online" or "original oil paintings for sale Cape Town," your gallery appears near the top of the results instead of a competitor's.

Your website is beautifully designed, has great product photography, and detailed descriptions of every artwork. The foundation is solid. The issue is that Google is currently missing some key signals that tell it what you sell, where you're based, and why collectors should buy from you. This report explains exactly what to fix.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you've curated the most beautiful gallery exhibition in Cape Town — but the gallery is on a side street with no signage, no listing in any art directory, and the door says "Shop" instead of "Art Gallery." The art inside is exceptional, but nobody knows it's there. SEO is the signage, the directory listing, and the open door that brings collectors in.

Term 1

Title Tags

The headline Google shows in search results. Like a gallery name plate — the first thing a collector sees before deciding whether to visit.

Term 2

Meta Descriptions

The short description under your title in Google results. Think of it as a two-sentence pitch that convinces someone to click your link over a competitor's.

Term 3

Headings

The bold text on your pages. Google reads these to understand what each section is about — they're not just for visitors.

Term 4

Schema / Structured Data

Invisible code that tells Google exactly what your business is, where you're based, and what you sell. Unlocks enhanced features in search results.

Priority 1
Urgent fixes — do these first

These changes will have the biggest immediate impact on how many people find your gallery through Google. All of them can be done in your Shopify admin panel — no coding required.

Urgent

None of your pages have Google snippet text

When someone searches for art online, Google shows a short description under each website link. Right now, every single page on your site has a blank description — so Google makes something up (usually poorly), and fewer people click through to your gallery.

Now
Google writes its own description — often the first random sentence on the page, which may not mention art, Cape Town, or what you sell.
Fix
Discover original South African art from established and emerging artists. Oil paintings, prints & mixed media shipped globally from Cape Town. Browse the collection.
Why this matters for sales This text is your advertisement in Google's search results. When a collector sees your listing next to a competitor's, this description is what convinces them to click yours. Mentioning "original South African art," "Cape Town," and "shipped globally" immediately tells people what you offer and that they can buy from anywhere.
Urgent

Your homepage title doesn't tell Google what you sell

The title tag is the main blue link text in Google results — it's the most important signal Google uses to decide when to show your site. Currently it just says "Partners Online Gallery," which tells Google nothing about art, South Africa, or what kind of gallery you are.

Now
Partners Online Gallery
Fix
Partners Online Gallery | South African Art for Sale | Cape Town
Why this matters for sales When someone searches "buy South African art online" or "original paintings for sale Cape Town," Google doesn't know to show your site because those words aren't in your title. Adding them is the single biggest ranking improvement you can make — and it takes 30 seconds in Shopify.
Urgent

Your homepage has a confusing structure for Google

Every webpage should have one main heading — like the title of a chapter in a book. Your homepage has two main headings: "Curated Online Art with Soul and Story" and "Partners Online Gallery." This confuses Google about what the page is actually about.

Now
Two competing main headings — neither mentions "South African art" or "paintings for sale"
Fix
One clear main heading: "Curated South African Art Online — Original Paintings & Prints"
Why this matters for sales "Curated Online Art with Soul and Story" sounds beautiful, but it's invisible to someone searching Google for South African art. Including the words collectors actually search for ("South African art," "original paintings," "prints") helps Google match your site to those searches — without losing the artistic feel.

Priority 2
Important improvements
Important

Your photos and banners don't have descriptions Google can read

Google cannot see images. It reads a hidden text label (called "alt text") to understand what each photo shows. Your artwork product images are well-described — great job there. However, your banner images, founder photos, and service icons have no descriptions at all.

Now
Banner images, your profile photos, and icons — Google sees blank space.
Fix
"David Vermeulen — Co-founder of Partners Online Gallery" / "South African art collection — original paintings and prints"
Why this matters for sales Google Images is a genuine traffic source for art galleries. When someone searches "South African landscape paintings" or "Cape Town artist," properly labelled images appear in image results and drive people to your site. This also improves accessibility for visually impaired visitors using screen readers.
Important

When people share your art on social media, it doesn't preview properly

When someone shares a link to one of your artworks on Facebook, WhatsApp, or Pinterest, the platform tries to show a preview with an image, title, and description. Your site is currently missing the code that controls this preview — so shared links may show a broken image, wrong text, or nothing at all.

Why this matters for sales Art is inherently visual and shareable. When a collector finds a piece they love and shares it with a friend, the preview needs to show the artwork beautifully with the title and price. Pinterest in particular is a major discovery channel for art — and you already have a Pinterest account. Fixing this makes every share work harder for you.
Important

Your About page also has a double-heading problem

Just like the homepage, your About page has two main headings: "Bringing Artists and Collectors Together" and "About Partners Online Gallery." Google only expects one main heading per page.

Now
Two main headings competing for attention.
Fix
One clear main heading: "About Partners Online Gallery — Connecting Collectors with South African Art"
Why this matters for sales Your story — David and Zanne's passion for SA art — builds trust. Google rewards pages that demonstrate expertise and authority (they call it "E-E-A-T"). A well-structured About page with clear headings helps Google understand you're a legitimate, expert gallery.

Priority 3
Unlocking Google's enhanced features
Important

Google doesn't know you're an art gallery

Your site tells Google it's an "Organisation" — which is technically true but very generic. It could be a bank, a school, or a plumber. There's specific code your developer can add that tells Google: "This is an art gallery, based in Cape Town, selling art from R1,500 to R92,000, founded by David Vermeulen and Zanne Cronjé."

What this unlocks for you Once added, your Google listing could show your price range, your location, and the types of art you sell — all visible before someone even clicks through to your site. This is a one-time setup that makes your search result stand out from competitors who show as plain text links.
Growth Opportunity
Content that brings collectors to you

Your site currently has no blog or editorial content. Every page is either a product listing or a static page. This means Google only has one type of content to work with. Adding a blog with articles about South African art, artist stories, and collecting guides creates dozens of new ways for people to discover your gallery.

"How to Buy Art Online in South Africa" — guide

Targets people actively searching for how to buy art online. Positions you as the trusted authority and naturally links to your collection.

Artist spotlight series — one post per artist

You have 13 featured artists, each with a unique story. "Meet Dawie Fourie" or "The Art of Karin Hopkinson" creates 13 new pages Google can rank — each targeting the artist's name as a search term.

"Why Invest in South African Art" — thought piece

Targets collectors searching for art as investment. Establishes your gallery's credibility and expertise in the South African art market.

FAQ page — shipping, framing, commissions

Answers the questions collectors ask before buying. Also helps Google show your site for question-based searches like "how to ship art internationally."

Even though you're an online-only gallery, a Google Business Profile (free) puts you on Google Maps and in local search results for "art gallery Cape Town." It also gives you a place to collect reviews, post updates, and appear in Google's knowledge panel.
